Handling several problems at once is a normal part of retail management, especially at peak times, and it’s one of the best ways to tell a strong manager from an average one. Anyone can stay polite with one calm customer; the real test is what they do when three things go wrong together.

Realistic “everything at once” situations
Scenario	What’s happening at the same time
Saturday rush	Angry customer on the phone + a staff member says the card machine is down + the queue is out the door
Short-staffed peak	2 staff called in sick + a delivery truck arrives early + a customer complains about waiting
Safety incident mid-complaint	Handling a complaint when a staff member shouts “someone slipped in aisle 3!”
Boss calls at the worst time	The area manager wants sales figures right now, while a VIP customer is standing at the counter
Suspected shoplifter during a sale	A staff member reports a possible shoplifter while the floor is packed and a customer is mid-complaint
Closing-time chaos	The till is short + an employee wants to leave early for an emergency + a customer arrives 2 minutes before closing
What this reveals
Skill	Strong candidate	Weak candidate
Triage	Deals with safety first, then money or operations, then complaints	Treats everything as equally urgent, or ignores the emergency
Delegation	“Priya, put out a wet-floor sign and call first aid, I’ll be there in 30 seconds”	Tries to do everything personally
Communication under interruption	“I’m so sorry, I need to put you on hold for 30 seconds for a safety issue. I’ll be right back.”	Hangs up, or ignores the colleague
Closing the loop	Comes back to the customer and finishes the job	Forgets the first problem entirely
Composure	Tone stays steady	Rushes, sounds stressed, snaps

Closing the loop is a strong signal judges will love: did the candidate remember and return to the first problem?

How to simulate it with voice

This would be your signature “Pressure Mode” scenario. Here’s how a 4-minute call would play out:

0:00  Angry customer (phone): "I was overcharged and I'm posting a review!"
0:45  ⚡ Staff member interrupts (different voice): "Card machine's down, queue's huge!"
1:30  ⚡ Staff member: "Someone slipped near aisle 3!"
2:30  Customer (if left on hold): "Hello?? Are you still there?"
3:30  Wrap-up: did they return and resolve the original complaint?

How I’d build it:

The angry customer is the live ElevenLabs agent, as planned.
The interruptions are short clips in a different voice (a staff member), played at set times. When one plays, the app tells the AI customer what just happened, so the customer reacts realistically: “Excuse me, are you even listening to me?”
The difficulty adapts. If the candidate copes well, the customer gets more impatient; if they’re struggling, events can be spaced out.
The scoring adds 3 criteria for this scenario: Prioritisation, Delegation and Closing the loop. The report shows a timeline of each interruption and how the candidate responded to it.
How it fits the product
Round 1: the 3 standard scenarios (customer, staff, ethics).
Round 2: if the owner picks “stress test on composure / prioritisation”, the candidate gets a Pressure Mode scenario.
For the demo: a judge does a Pressure Mode call live, then sees the timeline. That would be the high point of the presentation.

My recommendation: build Pressure Mode as a premium scenario, but after the core loop works (one call → score → report). It’s the most impressive feature but also the trickiest, and the timed interruptions need testing.
