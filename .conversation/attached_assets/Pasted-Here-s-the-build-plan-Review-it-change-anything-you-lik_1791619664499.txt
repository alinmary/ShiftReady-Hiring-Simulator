# Plan: "ShiftReady" (placeholder name), voice-simulation hiring for retail

## 1. How it works

**Candidate side**
1. The candidate opens an invite link, does a quick mic check and reads a short brief: "You're working the phone line at the store."
2. They go through **3 phone-call simulations**. An AI customer talks back in real time: interrupts, gets angry, calms down if handled well. Each call is capped at about 3–4 minutes.
3. They finish, and the report is generated automatically.

**Shop-owner side**
1. **Dashboard:** grouped by role (e.g. "Store Associate", "Category Manager"). Each role shows its top candidates ranked by score, not one huge list.
2. **Candidate report:**
   - Scores from 1 to 10 on each criterion
   - A verdict: Strong Hire, Decent Hire or Do Not Hire
   - **Highlights:** the moments they did well or fumbled, each with a timestamp. Clicking one plays that part of the audio.
   - The full transcript
   - Suggested follow-up questions for the next conversation
3. **Next round:** the owner types or ticks things like "stress-test escalation and composure". The system writes 3 new, harder scenarios aimed at those weak spots and sends the candidate a new link. Round 2 results appear next to round 1.

## 2. Scoring rubric (1–10 each)

| # | Criterion | What it checks | Based on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empathy & active listening | Acknowledges feelings, paraphrases, doesn't interrupt | Transcript + audio |
| 2 | Problem resolution | Offers a concrete, workable fix | Transcript |
| 3 | Escalation judgment | Escalates when needed, not too early or too late | Transcript |
| 4 | Policy & integrity | No false promises, follows rules, honest | Transcript |
| 5 | Communication clarity | Clear, structured, no jargon | Transcript |
| 6 | Vocal tone & warmth | Calm, warm, respectful tone; no sarcasm or curtness | Audio |
| 7 | Composure under pressure | Stays steady when the customer escalates | Audio + transcript |

**Verdict rules (you can change these):**
- **Strong Hire:** weighted average ≥ 8 and no criterion below 6
- **Decent Hire:** average 6–7.9
- **Do Not Hire:** average below 6, or any **red flag** (rudeness, lying to the customer, discriminatory remarks)

The AI only *recommends*. The owner makes the final call, which also matters legally for AI hiring tools.

## 3. Starting scenarios (all phone calls)

1. **Damaged order, angry customer:** wants a full refund plus compensation beyond policy. Tests empathy, resolution and policy.
2. **Overcharged and threatening a bad review:** demands the manager immediately. Tests escalation judgment and composure.
3. **Out-of-stock promise:** a regular customer was promised an item that isn't coming. They're disappointed rather than angry, which tests warmth and honesty.

I'll keep these in a scenario library so roles can mix and match, and round-2 scenarios are generated from it.

## 4. Technology and which model does what

**About audio:** Claude can't take audio files directly. So the plan is:
- ElevenLabs records the call and transcribes it with word-level timestamps.
- My code extracts **voice signals** from the recording: speaking pace, pauses, filler words, talking over the customer, loudness and pitch changes.
- Claude gets both the transcript and those signals, so tone and composure are scored on real audio evidence, not just the words.

| Job | Tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Real-time AI customer voice | **ElevenLabs Conversational AI agent** | Natural, low-latency voice with interruptions |
| The customer's "brain" during the call | **Claude Sonnet 5.5** (low effort) | Fast enough for live speech, still convincing |
| Scoring, highlights, verdict | **Claude Opus 5.5** (high effort) | The most important judgment; needs careful reasoning and consistent results |
| Round-2 scenario generation | **Claude Opus 5.5** | Has to write targeted, realistic scenarios |
| Claude Fable 5.1 | Not used | More than twice the cost of Opus and slower. Opus 5.5 handles this well. |

**Rest of the stack:**
- Next.js (TypeScript) app with Tailwind
- SQLite database: zero setup, good for a demo
- Audio stored locally
- Can deploy to Vercel later

**Rough cost:** about $0.20–0.40 of Claude usage per candidate, plus ElevenLabs credits for about 10–12 minutes of voice.

## 7. Ideas worth considering

- **Fairness:** "tone" should mean calmness, warmth and respect, **never** accent or fluency. I'll write this into the scoring instructions explicitly. Judges will notice, and it's the right call.
- **Score confidence:** if a candidate barely spoke or the audio was poor, the report says "low confidence – re-test" instead of giving a misleading score.
- **"Hear the best answer":** next to a fumble, play how the top candidate handled the same moment. That's a strong visual for the demo.
- **Optional candidate feedback:** candidates get a short, kind summary of how they did, which makes the product look good to them too.
